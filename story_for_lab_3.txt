The hum of the ancient amplifier vibrated through Lyra’s boots, but the sound that truly mattered was the quiet unraveling of the air around her hands.

Where others saw solid granite and cold iron, Lyra felt an infinite mosaic of tiny, movable points. With a shift of her focus, she could soften titanium into warm wax or weave atmospheric nitrogen into crystalline shields. For eighteen years, she had hidden this talent, altering minor things—turning stale bread soft, mending fractured bones in secret.

Now, standing at the heart of the Null Core—a failing planetary engine sinking toward the Earth’s mantle—the luxury of hiding was gone.

Above her, the surface sky burned a toxic violet. The atmospheric generators had collapsed, and the global collapse was hours away. She was the only entity capable of interacting with the Core on an atomic level.

She pressed her palms against the central pedestal. The sheer weight of the machine's broken lattice flooded her mind. It was a chaotic, rusted ruin. Rearranging its subatomic structure to stabilize the planet's magnetic field would consume every remaining drop of her physical energy, likely fusing her own body into the metal forever. She would save billions, but end herself.

Alternatively, she could pull.

If she reversed the binding forces instead of mending them, the Core would instantly collapse into a micro-singularity. The chain reaction would reduce the dying, corrupt world to dust in seconds—a clean, painless end to a century of suffering and war. No pain, no lingering extinction. Just silence.

Her fingertips glowed white as the atoms began to slip. The boundary between creation and void blurred on the palm of her hand.

Lyra closed her eyes, listened to the faint, distant rhythm of life miles above, and chose to push.
